Full stack refers to a developer who has the ability to work across all layers of the technology stack in building an application — from the frontend (user interface), backend (business logic and API), to the database and deployment infrastructure. In a broader context, full stack can also refer to technologies that comprehensively cover all of these layers. A useful mental model is the "T-shaped developer" — broad knowledge across the full stack (the horizontal bar of the T) with deep expertise in one or two areas (the vertical bar).

## Technology Layers

**Frontend** encompasses HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and frameworks such as React, Vue, or Astro. **Backend** involves server-side languages like Node.js, Python, Go, or PHP with their respective frameworks. **Database** includes relational systems (PostgreSQL, MySQL) and NoSQL (MongoDB, Redis). **DevOps & infrastructure** covers Docker, CI/CD, and cloud deployment. Full-stack frameworks like Next.js (React) and Django (Python) abstract away some of the layer boundaries, allowing developers to handle both frontend and backend logic within a single project structure.

## Role in a Team

Full stack developers are especially valuable in startups and small teams where one person needs to handle many aspects. They can move quickly from prototyping to deployment. In larger teams, specialists are still typically needed for critical areas such as database tuning or UI/UX, while the full stack developer maintains coherence across layers.

## Challenges

The range of technologies to learn is very wide — the risk of being a "jack of all trades, master of none" is very real. Full stack developers must strategically choose areas to deepen while maintaining a broad understanding of the entire stack.
