# Physical AI

Physical AI refers to AI systems that perceive, reason about and act in the physical world.

Robots, autonomous vehicles, industrial machines, drones and intelligent edge devices are common examples. A typical system combines perception, planning, control and feedback.

## How it works

**Perception** uses cameras, lidar, microphones, force sensors or other measurements.

**World understanding** estimates objects, positions, motion and relationships.

**Planning** selects actions against goals and constraints.

**Control** converts decisions into physical commands.

**Feedback** checks what actually happened and can trigger replanning.

## Practical example

A warehouse robot detects shelves and people, estimates their positions, plans a safe route, drives through it and continuously checks sensor feedback. Simulation can test many scenarios before deployment, but real-world validation remains necessary.

Physical AI is different from software-only AI because failures can have physical consequences. Latency, uncertainty, safety margins, hardware failures and rare events therefore matter greatly.

## Physical AI and world models

A world model can represent an environment and predict how it may change under actions. It can support physical AI, but physical AI does not require one particular world-model architecture.
